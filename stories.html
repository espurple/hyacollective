Fatigue & energy · France

“I was looking for this — everyone told me not to go.”

For about eight years, C. had eaten just one main meal a day. What followed was persistent fatigue and hair loss, and a stubborn knot of tension across her shoulders. She had wanted to try Chinese medicine for a long time, but had been told it would only ever be available in Chinese.

We arranged a consultation in English with Dr. Eileen — tongue and pulse diagnosis, facial and body acupuncture, ear seeds — and finished the afternoon blending her own herbal tea from astragalus, red dates and mulberries. She left visibly relaxed, with a formula to take home and a clear picture of how her eating pattern had been draining her energy.

I'm really impressed by how, just by looking, we can say that many things about the health of someone.

Facial acupuncture

Natural beauty, rooted in TCM.

Curious whether Chinese medicine could refresh her appearance — without surgery or injections — D. asked us to arrange facial acupuncture at a licensed Shanghai clinic. The physician "read" her face like a map, then placed dozens of very fine needles, paired with a gentle micro-current.

Half an hour later, D. described feeling lifted and awake — brighter eyes and a softer, more defined look; as she put it, her face had simply "let go." Her verdict, with a laugh: "Successful." Facial acupuncture is having a moment in beauty circles, but its roots run deep in traditional practice.

One guest's personal impression, not a guaranteed outcome; delivered by a licensed physician. Experiences vary.
